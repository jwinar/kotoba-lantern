# Data sources and their licenses

Everything under `assets/data/` is generated by `scripts/build_jlpt_data.py`
from the sources below. The generated files are derivative works, so they
carry the obligations of what they're derived from — this file is the record
of that, and the app repeats the short version on its Settings → *Fonts, data
& credits* screen.

## Vocabulary — `jlpt_n5.json` … `jlpt_n1.json`

The word lists come from
[open-anki-jlpt-decks](https://github.com/jamsinclair/open-anki-jlpt-decks)
(MIT), which took them from
[chyyran/jlpt-anki-decks](https://github.com/chyyran/jlpt-anki-decks), which
took them from **Jonathan Waller's JLPT resources at
[tanos.co.uk](https://www.tanos.co.uk/jlpt/)**, licensed
**Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY)**.

Attribution required: *JLPT vocabulary lists by Jonathan Waller, CC BY.*

**These lists are reconstructions, not the exam's own word list.** The JLPT
has published no official vocabulary list since the 2010 redesign, so level
boundaries here are well-established convention rather than an authority's
ruling. Words are assigned to exactly one level: where the sources overlap,
the easier level wins (see the generator's `claimed` set).

## Kanji detail — `kanji_info.json`

Stroke counts and on/kun readings come from
[davidluzgouveia/kanji-data](https://github.com/davidluzgouveia/kanji-data),
whose kanji fields derive from **KANJIDIC2**, © the
[Electronic Dictionary Research and Development Group](https://www.edrdg.org/),
licensed **CC BY-SA 4.0**.

Attribution and share-alike required: `kanji_info.json` is a derivative of
KANJIDIC2 and is itself **CC BY-SA 4.0**. Only the KANJIDIC-derived fields are
used — `strokes`, `readings_on`, `readings_kun`. The WaniKani-derived fields in
that source (`wk_*`) are deliberately untouched, since they are not offered
under the same terms.

## Example sentences — `sentences.json`

Hand-written for this app (150 sentences, one per curated N5 word), kept in
`scripts/curated_n5.json`. No third-party rights; same license as the app.

## Word class — derived, not sourced

The lists carry no part of speech. `infer_part_of_speech` in the generator
adds one for verbs only, and only where the dictionary form proves it: a
gloss beginning "to " marks a verb, anything not ending in る is then
unambiguously godan, and る-verbs use the i/e-row vowel rule plus an explicit
list of common godan exceptions (帰る, 走る, 知る …). Adjectives are left
alone on purpose — i- and na-adjectives read identically in English, and
嫌い/きれい end in い while conjugating as na-adjectives.

This is our own derivation, not third-party data, and carries no additional
licence obligation.

## What this means in practice

- Keep the attributions screen accurate if the sources change.
- `kanji_info.json` may be redistributed, including commercially, provided
  EDRDG is credited and the file stays under CC BY-SA 4.0.
- The app's own code, the curated sentences and the generator are not
  affected by share-alike — only the generated kanji data is.
